## Kick B

| Check | Tip | Source | Quote or paraphrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| how (wrong sound) | Press your lips together, build pressure, and let them burst open on a "b". No vowel after it. | HB-B, SB 8:58, MG | HB-B: "say the letter 'b' but force the air out ... building up a little pressure behind your lips". SB: "When you say the B, release the tension in your lips. It's like an explosion." MG: "You're building up pressure in your mouth and then releasing it." |
| loud | Louder. Make the "b" tight and powerful, or move closer to the mic. | MG | "Make it as tight, powerful, and loud as possible." The mic part is not from a tutorial (see notes). |
| vowel | No "buh". Keep only the lip pop and stop before any vowel. Think "boo" without the "oo". | HB-B, MG, FK 0:40, SK 1:37, SB 9:08 | HB-B (Fat Tony): "Say the word "boo". Then take away the "oo"". MG: "Remove the oo and keep the B." FK: "now let's do it without [the vowel]". SK: "now just do the kick drum without the e". SB: "Now let's remove the vowels and emphasize the B." |
| air | Less air. Use the pressure in your mouth, not your lungs, for one short pop. | FK 0:28 to 0:33 | "you use the muscles of your mouth and then you say B with a lot of pressure ... without using the lungs". |
| low | Deeper. Press the insides of your lips together, purse them a little, and pop from the center. | MG, SK 1:43, SB 8:54 and 9:42 | MG: "If you release it from the front of your lips (with the outside of your lips touching), it sounds like a P. If you touch the insides of your lips together and purse your lips slightly, it sounds like a B ... This is where you create the bass sound". SK: "by changing the shape of your lips you will also change the frequency. I personally do it in the center of my lips in a round shape". SB: "Purse your lips", "Avoid making a fish mouth with your lips turned in." |
| punch | Sharper. Build up pressure behind closed lips, then release it all at once, like an explosion. | HB-B, SB 8:58, SK 1:30 | HB-B: "Get this punchier by building up a little pressure behind your lips". SB: "release the tension in your lips. It's like an explosion." SK: "emphasize it a little bit with more pressure from the lips". |
| veto | Not a kick: no low end. | MG | MG calls the B "the bass sound". The veto fires when the attack has almost no energy below 200 Hz. |

## Closed hi-hat t

| Check | Tip | Source | Quote or paraphrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| how (wrong sound) | Put your tongue tip behind your top front teeth and snap it down on a short, whispered "t". | SB 3:25 and 3:35, FH 0:14 and 0:24, HB-t | SB: "Just lift the tip of your tongue and place it behind your front teeth ... Make a TS sound." FH: "you do it by saying the T ... putting your tongue behind your front teeth and you say T". HB-t: "Remove the vocalization ... releasing a short burst of air". |
| loud | Louder. Build a little pressure behind your tongue tip, then snap it off, or move closer to the mic. | HB-t | "focus on strengthening the consonant sound of "t". You may create this sound by breathing outward or just building pressure in your mouth and releasing a short burst of air". |
| vowel | No "tuh". Keep only the tongue tap, like the "t" at the end of "hat". | HB-t | "making the "t" sound as if you were saying "hat" or "cat." Remove the vocalization and focus on strengthening the consonant sound". |
| air | Shorter. Snap the "t" off and stop the air. A long "tsss" is an open hi-hat. | HB-t, SB 3:14 | HB-t: "releasing a short burst of air. Follow through with an "s" sound to create an open hi hat effect". SB contrasts "the closed high hat or the opened high hat". |
| voice | Whisper the "t", with no voice. A hand on your throat should feel no buzz. | HB-t | "Remove the vocalization". The hand-on-throat check is mine, not from a tutorial (see notes). |
| veto | Not a hi-hat: no high hiss. | (rule wording) | Fires when the attack's spectral centroid is under 2 kHz. Not from a tutorial. |

## Notes and open questions for the owner

1. **"Move closer to the mic"** in every `loud` tip is not from a tutorial. The loudness check reads dBFS, so mic distance and gain matter as much as the mouth. Keep it, or split it into a separate setup hint?
2. **Hand on the throat** (both `voice` tips) is a standard phonetics check for voicing, not something these tutorials say. They only say "remove the vocalization". I think it is the most useful part of the tip for a beginner, but it is my addition.
3. **Conflict, K air.** SkilleR (2:49 to 3:14) says to "drag it a little bit", make it "more crispy and crunchy", and "you also can exhale a little bit more air". FootboxG says "almost no air". The tip follows FootboxG. If SkilleR's K clips fail the air check, the audit will flag the rule, and the tip may need softening ("a small puff" rather than "no air").
4. **Conflict, voice on t.** SkilleR (2:14 to 2:36) shows a tongue-only hi-hat where "you can use your voice on top". That is a different, layered technique. The tip follows HUMAN BEATBOX ("Remove the vocalization") for the classic t.
5. **Conflict, B air.** MusicGurus suggests, once the basic B works, to "use your diaphragm to push out even more air" for power. FootboxG says the opposite ("without using the lungs"). The `air` check measures the hissy tail after the pop, so the tip follows FootboxG. Worth a listen against the pro kicks.
6. **"Snap it down"** for the t (how) and "snap it off" (loud) are my wording for the release. The tutorials only say "place it behind your front teeth" and "a short burst of air".
7. HUMAN BEATBOX's "Classic Kick Drum" page also suggests a very short lip vibration ("a bit like 'blow a raspberry'") for a louder kick. I left it out because a beginner may turn it into a lip roll, which the judge would hear as a long, voiced tail.
8. Web search for "common beginner mistakes" mostly turned up low-quality SEO pages (schoolofbeatbox.com reads machine-written and calls the K snare "inward"). I did not use them.
